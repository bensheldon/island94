This morning’s print edition of The New York Times Magazine has a long cover story by me titled “Is Air Travel Getting Less Safe?”

The story’s main argument is that the culture and institutions that have made US air travel exceptionally safe are at fundamental odds with the culture and practices of DOGE/MAGA/Project 2025. To wit:

Aviation safety depends on ruthless honesty about risks, mistakes, and failures. On a refusal to make excuses or shift blame. On respect and protection for those who ask “What if?” or say “Hey, wait a minute.” On transparency and coordination among the diverse people and institutions who share responsibility for life-and-death stakes. All of whom take that burden seriously.

And by contrast:

The DOGE/MAGA regime features impatience with collaboration and coordination, contempt for bureaucracy or institutional memory, glee at wielding the chainsaw and seeing how things look once the dust has blown away. On quick fixes, snarky videos, and an insistence on always having been right.

Overall this is “move fast and break things,” versus “better safe than sorry.”
