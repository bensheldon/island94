How Could That Possibly Work?

https://deprogrammaticaipsum.com/how-could-that-possibly-work/

November 16, 2026

In many ways, this period coincides with the death of software engineering methodology. Methodology is the study of method; not the engineering of software, but the engineering of software engineering. The people who went on that famous Snowbird skiing holiday that generated the Agile manifesto were a mix of people who make software, people who manage people who make software, people who tell other people how to make software, and people who think about how people go about making software; with that last group being the methodologists. These people compared notes on the ways in which they worked, which were all biased towards so-called “lightweight methods” that optimise for learning what people want, making software that does that, then checking whether anything’s changed. What they came up with was a document that said to do that. Get software out to your customer as quickly as you can, work with them to understand what to do next, and do not be afraid of finding out that they (now) want something else.

That is such broadly applicable (and vague) advice that it has never been superseded. Everyone thinks they are doing that, whether they are following by-the-book Scrum, a poorly understood Scrumbut process, or any of the other Lean, XP, Kanban etc. processes that you come across. The only real attempts at modernising methodology in the quarter-decade since Agile killed it have been devops and continuous delivery, and those have both been restatements of the ideas of agile software development.
